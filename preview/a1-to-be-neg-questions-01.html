Verb To Be — Negatives & Questions

A1 — grammar, listening, reading, writing

Common mistake: “You are a teacher?” — Correct: “Are you a teacher?”

To ask a question with 'to be' we put the verb first (Are you...?), and to make it negative we add 'not' after the verb (She is not...).

  • Ben is tired after his trip.
  • Sofía and Kenji are ready for class.
  • Ben is not from Colombia. He is not tired now.
  • They are not at home. I am not late.
  • She isn't Japanese, and we aren't teachers.
  • Are you ready? Is Ben tired?
  • Where is Sofía? Why are they late?
  • Are you a student? Yes, I am.
  • Is Ben Colombian? No, he isn't.
  • Are they ready? No, they aren't.

Practice

  1. ___ you ready for the test? (Is, Are, Am)
  2. Which question is correct? (Ben is British?, Is Ben British?, British is Ben?)
  3. To make a question with 'to be', we put the verb before the subject: 'Are you...?'.
  4. ___ Sofía from Bogotá? No, she is from Medellín.
  5. We ___ not at home right now.
  6. Unscramble: you, Are, tired, ?
  7. Find the mistake: She no is Japanese.
  8. Kenji is a student. — Turn it into a question.
  9. They are ready. — Make it negative (use 'not').
  10. Are you a teacher? (Answer: Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.)

Listening

Kenji: Are you ready for the test, Ben?

Ben: No, I'm not! I am very nervous.

Kenji: Is it difficult?

Ben: It is not easy. Are you nervous too?

Kenji: No, I'm not. We are ready, don't worry.

Reading

Ben is not Colombian; he is British. Today he is tired because he is not used to the heat. 'Are you okay, Ben?' asks Sofía. 'I am not sick,' he says, 'but I am very hot!' Sofía laughs. They are good friends, and they are happy in Medellín.

  • Ben is British.
  • Ben is sick.
  • Sofía and Ben are friends.
